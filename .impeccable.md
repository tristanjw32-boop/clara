## Design Context

### Users
Small business owners ($500K–$5M revenue) in trades, consulting, retail, and services. They arrive via their AI assistant (Hermes, OpenClaw) or an accountant referral. They have no CFO, no finance team, and no time. They've been burned by dashboards they never opened. They're not looking for another tool — they're looking for someone who understands their books and tells them what to do.

### Brand Personality
**Honest · Direct · Warm.** Clara should feel like a trusted CFO who picks up the phone — not a SaaS product, not a fintech dashboard. Authoritative without being intimidating. Plain-spoken without being simplistic. The emotional goal: confidence and calm from someone who actually knows your business.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial CFO. Think Financial Times personal finance section, not a fintech startup. Warm off-white backgrounds (#F7F6F2), deep forest green accent (#1E5739), warm near-black text (#1C1C18). Typography: Instrument Serif for display headings (warmth, authority), Inter for body (clarity, precision). Left-aligned, asymmetric layouts. Generous whitespace. No cards, no shadows, no gradients, no glassmorphism.

Anti-references: ShieldReady's cold corporate blue palette, generic SaaS gradient heroes, glassmorphism cards, hero metric dashboards with big numbers and glow accents.

### Design Principles
1. **Show, don't promise.** Lead with a real Clara output snippet, not marketing claims. SMB owners trust evidence over copy.
2. **Direct to the point of discomfort.** "Your cash drops below payroll threshold in 31 days" — not "gain visibility into your financial health."
3. **No chrome, only signal.** Strip everything decorative that doesn't carry meaning. Every visual element earns its place.
4. **Brand register.** This is a landing page — design IS the product. It must feel like something you'd trust with your books.
5. **Warmth through typography.** Instrument Serif at display scale does the emotional heavy lifting. Let it breathe.
