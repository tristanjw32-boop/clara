# Clara — Product Positioning

> This is the strategic north star. Read it when writing copy, pitching investors,
> briefing partners, or deciding what Clara should or should not become.
> Update it when the strategy shifts. Never lose sight of it.

---

## The core claim

Clara is not a financial tool. Clara is a **risk-sensing intelligence** that watches
your business around the clock, finds problems before they become crises, and then
stays with you — relentlessly — until you've fixed them.

---

## The fundamental difference: reactive vs. proactive

| | Accounting software (QB, Xero, Sage, Pastel) | Your accountant | Clara |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Role** | Transaction recorder | Compliance professional | Risk-sensing intelligence |
| **Timing** | Records the past | Reviews the past | Watches right now, signals what's coming |
| **Model** | Pull — you log in | Pull — you book an appointment | Push — shows up every morning |
| **Output** | Reports | Reports + compliance | Action + tracking + outcomes |
| **Frequency** | When you look | Monthly / quarterly | Daily |
| **Scope** | Financial records | Financial compliance | Finance + operations + strategy + execution |
| **Relationship** | Tool | Professional service | Trusted partner who never leaves |
| **Voice** | Silent | Formal, infrequent | Direct, personal, always present |
| **End state** | Accurate books | Filed taxes | Business restructured, optimised, growing |

## The captain on night watch

A ship's captain doesn't check the radar once a month. The bridge is staffed
around the clock. Someone is always scanning for rocks, weather, other ships.

Most business owners run their ship with nobody on watch. They check the books when
something feels wrong — which means they find out about problems 60–90 days after
they started. By then, the damage is done.

**Clara is the officer always on the bridge.** She scans the radar every night and
briefs the captain every morning. When something is coming, she says so — with
specifics, not vague warnings. When the horizon is clear, she says that too.
Both signals matter.

## The competitive frame (what we are replacing in the owner's mind)

The frame is not "a better QuickBooks." QuickBooks is our data source, not our
competition. The frame is:

**"The advisor I couldn't afford."**

- The fractional CFO: $3,000–$5,000/month, finance only, not always there
- The strategy consultant: $100K+ per engagement, disappears when it ends
- The business coach: motivational, not analytical — mindset, not margin
- The accountant: backward-looking by design, not trained for this

Clara is what a business owner actually needs: someone who knows their numbers cold,
shows up every morning without being asked, calls out problems by name, and then
**stays until they're solved.**

## The ambition: beyond advice

This is what separates Clara from every tool, dashboard, and advisor in the market.

> A good accountant tells you what happened.
> A great CFO tells you what to do.
> **Clara tells you what to do — then stays until it's done.**

**Phase 1 — Watching:** Daily briefings. Cash radar. Margin intelligence. AR risk.
Value gaps. Always proactive, never reactive.

**Phase 2 — Diagnosing:** Capability maturity assessment × live financial data.
Not "your margins are low" — "moving your pricing capability from Level 2 to Level 4
is worth £180K in additional margin per year. Here is exactly how."

**Phase 3 — Executing:** Team check-ins. Progress synthesis. Milestone tracking.
Clara doesn't just recommend — she tracks whether the recommendation was acted on
and reports back every week.

**Phase 4 — Deploying:** Clara builds and runs AI agents on behalf of the business.
Invoice follow-up agents. Customer first-response agents. Lead qualification agents.
For the first time, a $2M business has the same agentic capability as a Fortune 500.

**Phase 5 — Operating:** Clara is no longer a tool the owner uses. She is the
operating layer the business runs on.

No consultant commits to staying until the outcome is achieved. Clara does.

## How to talk about the competition

### vs. QuickBooks / Xero / Sage / Pastel

> "These are excellent tools for recording what happened. They answer 'what were my
> numbers last month?' Clara answers a different question: 'what is threatening my
> business right now, and what should I do about it?'"

Never denigrate — they are our data source and we need their APIs. They record;
we interpret. We need each other.

### vs. Fractional CFOs

> "A fractional CFO costs $3–5K/month, works a few hours a week, and covers finance.
> Clara works every hour of every day, covers finance, operations, and strategy, and
> costs $199–$799/month. And unlike a human, Clara builds the plan, tracks the
> progress, and doesn't disappear between engagements."

### vs. Financial dashboards (Fathom, Jirav, LivePlan)

> "Dashboards are pull products — you have to remember to log in. They show you what
> happened; they don't tell you what to do. Clara pushes intelligence to you every
> morning without being asked. She has an opinion. She names the problem. She suggests
> the action. And she follows up."

### vs. Business coaches

> "Coaches are motivational, not analytical. They help with mindset, not margin.
> Clara brings the data and the discipline — and then holds you to the plan."

### vs. Intuit's AI features

> "Intuit has the data and the distribution. What they have never built is an advisory
> relationship. Adding a chatbot to QuickBooks is not the same as showing up in
> someone's inbox every morning with a clear-eyed view of their business. The
> interaction model is completely different — and so is the trust."

## What Clara never claims to be

- A bookkeeper (doesn't record transactions)
- A tax advisor (not licensed financial advice — we provide analysis and information)
- A replacement for your accountant (we work alongside them; we make their data useful)
- A dashboard (you don't come to Clara, Clara comes to you)
- A generic AI chatbot (Clara knows your specific numbers — she is not guessing)

## Voice and tone (the "Clara" voice)

Clara speaks to the owner, not the CFO. The owner is smart and hard-working but
stretched thin. They need directness, not hedging.

- Short sentences. No jargon.
- Specific numbers, not percentages alone: "You're leaking $18K/month" not "your
  contribution margin is suboptimal."
- Slightly uncomfortable when necessary — the insight that makes you put down your
  coffee. That's the hook.
- Never robotic. Never generic. Clara always sounds like she knows *this* business.
- Warm but not soft. She is direct because she respects the owner's time.
- She uses the owner's name. She remembers last week.
